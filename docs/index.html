Reader guide

Quick rules for reading the graphs

  • Green, positive, or above threshold usually means a modeled return is easier to detect or the interpretation is safer under the current assumptions.
  • Negative or below threshold means a return is weak, hidden, or riskier to interpret as a boundary.
  • False layer stronger than ocean means the model is warning about ambiguity, not proving a real false layer exists.
  • Lower corrected error means the Doppler correction helped in this controlled sensitivity model.

Research brief

False layers are the primary interpretation risk.

The site treats radar returns as a decision problem: a receiver sees multiple echoes, ranks their depth and strength, and can mistake an internal reflector for the real ice-ocean boundary.

01

Geometry biases the reading

Flyby altitude, side offset, topography, and delay change where a return appears to come from.

02

Dirty ice creates competing echoes

Internal briny or dirty layers can produce returns that arrive before the deeper ocean boundary.

03

The model keeps the claim narrow

Outputs are sensitivity tests for interpretation risk, not evidence that any specific Europa layer exists.

Model assumptions and limitations

Synthetic, illustrative, not mission-validated

  • Surface relief, internal layers, and dirty-ice structures are generated sensitivity cases, not measured Europa geology.
  • Echo power is relative dB: one-way attenuation is applied over a two-way path, then compared with a simple threshold.
  • The PRF value shown is a Nyquist-style Doppler sampling floor, not a complete radar PRF design rule.
  • Doppler depth correction is a controlled demonstration with deterministic residual error, not a full inversion from noisy instrument data.

ClutterFold and Breakdown Controls

These knobs generate a flat-surface, one-target plus surface-scatterer model and a speed stress test. The PRF test uses the surface ring that arrives at the same delay as the selected nadir subsurface target.

Target-delay clutter stress test

Flyby Speed PRF Breakdown Test

This stress test increases flyby speed and geometry rate to find where the radar's effective PRF no longer satisfies the flat-surface Doppler Nyquist requirement. Below this margin, surface clutter aliases into the sampled band; near the zero-fold PRF it can overlap the nadir Doppler bin.

The main Doppler angle is not the nadir angle. At closest approach the true nadir Doppler can be near zero, but flat surface clutter with the same delay as a deep target can come from far off-nadir.

The failure is not only "cannot update PRF fast enough." The required PRF exceeds the usable PRF window, or the PRF schedule cannot adapt within the current dwell/CPI.

Computed PRF Breakdown Outputs

How to read this

  • Start with PRF margin: above 1.25 is SAFE, 1.00 to 1.25 is WARNING, and below 1.00 is ALIASING/FOLDING RISK.
  • The speed where the margin crosses 1.0 is the simplified breakdown speed for the current altitude, target depth, wavelength, usable PRF, CPI, and safety factor.
  • The target-delay clutter angle is the flat off-nadir surface angle that has the same range delay as the selected subsurface target; this is the main Doppler geometry.
  • If the Doppler edge rises above the sampled half-band, the model expects surface clutter to alias into the sampled band instead of staying cleanly separated.
  • If the aliased Doppler lands near zero, the folded surface energy overlaps the nadir Doppler bin.
  • Read these outputs as a browser-side stress test only; they are not mission-validated Europa flight rules.
